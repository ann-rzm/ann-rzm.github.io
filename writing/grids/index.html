Environment is like a grid — transparent and visible, but not necessarily traversable. Every grid takes itself as normal, and thinking only that way makes looking at others like watching fish through glass.

Small is good — at least it means the surroundings are big enough. But maybe that is not original. “Dignity” needs space — without physical space there is no psychological space; without psychological space there is only instinct left.

The capacity to cross grids may be necessary, but it is hard; the victory of the grid is the reality. The world is large enough that its whole is never seen; and small enough that several grids can be bumped into in one day.

The human essence is the ensemble of social relations — and structure is not in the business of favoring anyone.
