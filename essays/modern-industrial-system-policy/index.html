Policy Proposals for a Modern Industrial System

Abstract — Applied recommendations for shared industrial platforms, innovation environments, public data, domestic demand, and people-centered rural modernization.

Ann Rongze MaoFull research paper
On this pageResearch overviewFull paperResearch context
RESEARCH ABSTRACT

Overview

Applied recommendations for shared industrial platforms, innovation environments, public data, domestic demand, and people-centered rural modernization.

China’s extensive base of traditional industries is not only a stabilizer of employment but also the starting point for our progress toward modernization. The government could guide the establishment of a number of industry-wide shared digital platforms in distinctive industrial clusters, jointly with leading enterprises, to provide systematic digital empowerment and value-chain coordination. This would allow small and micro enterprises to access advanced R&D and design, supply-chain management, and intelligent-manufacturing tools at extremely low cost, much as they access infrastructure. To some extent, this could address firms’ deeper concerns about being unwilling or afraid to transform more effectively than subsidies alone, fundamentally improving the efficiency and resilience of entire industrial sectors. At the same time, leading firms in industrial chains should be encouraged and appropriately regulated to open innovation scenarios to upstream and downstream partners, fostering collaborative innovation in which large firms support small ones and small firms, in turn, benefit large ones, thereby turning our industrial-chain advantages into advantages in innovation ecosystems.

In cultivating emerging industries, policy should shift its emphasis from selecting priority fields toward creating an environment for growth. Emerging industries are characterized above all by uncertainty about technological pathways and market prospects. Balancing “planned innovation” with “exploration beyond the plan” requires the government to act as a thoughtful provider of application scenarios and designer of rules. To open up future-use scenarios for national development, government departments and large state-owned enterprises could serve as first users and proactively announce a set of frontier technology needs. Clear procurement commitments would provide new technologies and products with valuable early application scenarios and opportunities for iteration. It is crucial to cultivate the conditions for patient capital. Rather than investing directly, policymakers could consider optimizing the tax system and facilitating exit channels so that long-term social capital is more willing and able to flow into technological innovation. Only when capital gives innovation more time and latitude can we hope to obtain genuinely original and disruptive results more efficiently.

Measures to Further Advance the Development of Digital China

The two core drivers of future development are digital technology and domestic-demand potential. Digital technology should be used to facilitate the domestic economic cycle, while market demand should guide digital innovation.

In advancing Digital China, the emphasis should shift from building hardware to putting it to use, allowing data, as a new factor of production, to circulate and create value. Pilot programs for the authorized operation of public data could first be launched in fields rich in data resources, such as healthcare, transportation, and finance. Subject to strict safeguards for privacy and national security, secure technologies could be used to provide qualified enterprises and research institutions with limited access to public data. This could stimulate business-model innovation and research efficiency while offering broadly accessible data support to help address the digital divide. Meanwhile, leading firms in various industries should be encouraged to build industrial-internet platforms and make their digital capabilities available to small and medium-sized upstream and downstream enterprises through standardized modules and interfaces. This would support coordinated digital transformation across value chains and systematically improve the competitiveness of entire industrial ecosystems.

Of particular importance, Digital China and a strong domestic market are mutually reinforcing parts of an integrated whole. A strong domestic market offers an unparalleled testing ground and application space for digital innovation. Any digital technology that can be validated and iteratively improved in the Chinese market has a solid foundation for going global. In turn, the deep application of digital technology can substantially reduce market transaction costs, create new forms of goods and services, and unlock new consumption potential, continually expanding the domestic market. Digital technology should be used to empower every link in the domestic economic cycle, while the strength of the domestic market should nurture and refine China’s digital technology industries. In this way, we can chart a path toward high-quality development with Chinese characteristics.

Accelerating Agricultural and Rural Modernization

In advancing agricultural and rural modernization, the central question has shifted from whether supply is sufficient to whether it is good enough and offers sufficient value. Policy should therefore move beyond production subsidies alone and seek to activate underused rural resources. For example, orderly withdrawal from homestead-use rights and mechanisms for their transfer across regions could be explored. Pilot programs could be conducted in metropolitan areas with strong growth prospects and a high capacity to absorb population, allowing farmers who settle in cities to convert the use rights to idle rural homesteads into cultivated-land reclamation or construction-land quotas and trade them in a broader market. The proceeds should primarily support farmers as they settle in urban areas, providing solid social-security and housing support and turning rural “dead assets” into “active capital” that enables farmers to move to cities.

At the same time, people-centered new urbanization should focus on social integration and improvements in quality of life. Hundreds of millions of rural-to-urban migrants remain in a state of “semi-urbanization.” They represent a major reservoir of potential domestic demand and a critical point for social integration. Urbanization policy should therefore become more refined and more people-centered. A system should be introduced to provide full coverage of basic public services through the residence permit, decoupling public services from household-registration status so that services follow people. The equal right of accompanying children to receive education in the places where their families have migrated should be protected.

Agricultural and rural modernization and new urbanization should be planned as parts of a single whole. Agricultural and rural modernization provides a stable foundation and factor support for urbanization, while urbanization offers a destination for population transfers and a source of market demand. Strong interdepartmental coordination mechanisms should be established during implementation. For example, the allocation of newly added construction-land quotas in a region should be linked not only to the number of rural-to-urban migrants who obtain local household registration, but also to the actual provision of public services, including affordable housing, school places, and hospital beds, thereby creating a positive incentive loop.

Research context

This paper develops its central question through a defined mechanism, explicit methodological boundaries, and a concluding account of its public significance.
