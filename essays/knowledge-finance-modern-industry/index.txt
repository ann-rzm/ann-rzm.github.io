4:T40c,The University of Science and Technology of China has explored an innovative model for commercializing scientific and technological achievements that combines the allocation of rights, transfer, and agreed returns. It is analogous to the university issuing a “call option” to a research team: the team obtains ownership of the achievement, while the university retains certain rights to future value appreciation. The design has several theoretical advantages. First, linking the university’s returns closely to the future market value of the achievement helps address the difficulty of early-stage valuation. Second, clearly defining the research team’s rights and responsibilities can incentivize and sustain the industrialization of research results. Third, clear intellectual-property ownership and return expectations create more favorable conditions for risk-capital investment. This model can thus be regarded as a meaningful institutional innovation for addressing financing at the “first mile” of commercialization.5:T47b,Black Lake Technologies’ industrial-internet platform connects the production processes of tens of thousands of factories and aggregates large volumes of data in real time, ranging from production workflows to equipment status. This “data-rich resource” creates new possibilities for financial innovation; continuous data flows themselves hold substantial potential as new risk-control tools. In theory, financial institutions could use these data to tailor financial products for small and medium-sized manufacturers on the platform, linking credit limits and interest rates to the value of firms’ data assets—for example, improvements in production efficiency and order stability. In practice, one important reason some industrial funds invested in Black Lake was their confidence in the future value of its “industrial digital ecosystem.” They anticipated that it could serve as an entry point for expanding into broader financial markets, including supply-chain finance and equipment-financing leases. In other words, financial services could potentially expand from an individual factory to an entire digital production network.6:T4a7,This suggests that, while ensuring static “supply-chain security,” financial services also face an inherent need to promote the dynamic expansion of “knowledge-chain coordination.” This aligns with the strategic direction of the Recommendations. To implement the directives to strengthen enterprises’ principal role in technological innovation and improve industrial- and supply-chain resilience, finance for innovation chains may be a promising approach. For example, financing costs for chain-leading firms could be linked closely to the overall innovation performance of the industrial chain, measured by indicators such as the number of collaborative R&D projects or jointly held patents. In theory, this could create positive incentives to open knowledge interfaces. Building on reliable collaboration data accumulated by platforms, more precise credit services for supporting enterprises could also be explored.
