RESEARCH ABSTRACT

Overview

A firm-level case examining how manufacturing experience becomes data, data becomes an asset, and digital tools become part of a wider industrial ecosystem.

I. Introduction

The Recommendations of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China on Formulating the Fifteenth Five-Year Plan for National Economic and Social Development, adopted at the Fourth Plenary Session of the 20th Central Committee (hereinafter, the “Recommendations”), identify building a modernized industrial system and developing Digital China as parallel core strategic tasks. General Secretary Xi Jinping’s important discussions of new quality productive forces have provided direction for building a modernized industrial system. Against this background, the financial system’s core task is shifting away from expansion in scale and toward improving efficiency, acting as a “catalyst” for the deep integration of knowledge factors and industrial innovation.

The source of modern economic growth lies in the deep integration of knowledge and disruptive innovation. China enjoys two inherent advantages: a “complete industrial system” and an “ultra-large market.” Yet the future performance of the industrial system will depend critically on the efficiency with which knowledge circulates and is converted into practical applications within it. A financial system that can accurately identify innovative actors and provide them with effective support is like a system that accelerates the circulation of blood.

Notably, the scale of loans collateralized by data assets has grown rapidly since 2025. At the outset of the Fifteenth Five-Year Plan period, several questions arise: how can data factors be converted into assets that can genuinely serve as collateral through financial innovation? And how can traditional manufacturing firms use digital tools to overcome financing bottlenecks? Drawing on Black Lake Technologies’ practices, this article examines the pathways and financial logic through which data can be turned into assets.

Black Lake Technologies’ practices are highly consistent with the central ideas of the Recommendations and offer a micro-level case of developing new quality productive forces. Through the sequence of “turning data into a factor of production, factors into assets, and assets into credit,” the company has helped address traditional manufacturing’s financing difficulties and bottlenecks in transformation and upgrading. It offers a reference model of a “digital ecosystem” for financial support for a modernized industrial system.

II. From Craft Knowledge to Collateralizable Assets

In General Secretary Xi Jinping’s important discussions of new quality productive forces, a substantial increase in total factor productivity is a defining characteristic. Improving the effectiveness of translating scientific and technological achievements into applications is a key way to raise total factor productivity. The Recommendations for the Fifteenth Five-Year Plan state that new quality productive forces arise from “revolutionary breakthroughs in technology, innovative allocation of factors of production, and in-depth industrial transformation and upgrading.” Black Lake’s practices align with this macro-level strategy. Against a knowledge-driven backdrop, industry and finance can be integrated through innovation.

A. From Experience to Data: Digitizing Production Processes

The Recommendations place “promoting the intelligent and digital transformation of manufacturing” alongside “implementing initiatives for innovative development of the industrial internet” as key priorities. The starting point for the “high-tech” and “high-efficiency” attributes of new quality productive forces is to move beyond dependence on traditional production factors and upgrade the means of labor.

Traditional manufacturing often relies on experienced workers’ judgments and handwritten records, leaving knowledge embedded in individuals. Through its “Small Work Order” system, Black Lake moves the entire process—from sales orders and raw-material procurement to shop-floor production and finished-goods shipment—to the cloud. Workers scan codes to report production, and warehouse staff scan goods in and out; each operation becomes real-time data. This makes tacit knowledge explicit and preserves craft experience, laying the foundation for data assets to be developed.

From the perspective of production tools, this changes production management from a vague “art” dependent on personal experience into a precise, data-driven “science.” This is one manifestation of what is “new” about new quality productive forces. By using digital technology as a new source of momentum to transform the underlying operating logic of traditional productivity, it establishes the technological preconditions for higher total factor productivity.

B. From Resources to Assets: Factor Governance

The Recommendations emphasize improving the foundational institutions governing data factors. At its core, this means innovatively allocating factors of production so that data—the most dynamic of them—can enter the process of value creation as smoothly as land and capital.

From the perspective of financial innovation, the key feature of the Black Lake model is the “assetization” of data. The company does not itself produce the underlying production data; rather, it plays an effective role in integrating it.

Black Lake cleans, integrates, and standardizes data from different stages of production to create continuous, complete, and verifiable data flows, addressing data-quality problems. By bringing in qualified third-party assessment institutions, operating indicators such as order-completion rates and equipment-utilization rates can be quantified into a unified “credit score.” This serves as a bridge between industrial data and financial credit, supporting the integration of industry and finance.

The “pawnshop mentality” of traditional lending stems from banks’ inability to obtain a real-time and reliable picture of a firm’s operating conditions. Trustworthy data flows allow banks to see beyond the firm’s organizational boundaries, shifting from static “history” to monitoring a dynamic “flow.” Assessment moves away from dependence on collateral (stocks of assets) and toward the firm’s capacity to generate future cash flow.

This is the “quality” dimension of new quality productive forces. Value creation no longer rests solely on traditional physical capital; it also depends on the more efficient allocation of intangible factors such as knowledge and data—that is, on an orientation toward efficiency. More efficient and higher-quality economic activity can act as a fresh, vital flow, substantially improving total factor productivity.

C. From Tools to Ecosystems: Industrial Upgrading Through the Evolution of Financing

The Recommendations call for strengthening enterprises’ principal role in technological innovation and promoting integrated innovation across the upstream, midstream, and downstream segments of industrial chains. This points to a shift in industrial upgrading: from pursuing operational excellence within individual firms to collaborative co-development across entire industrial ecosystems. Moving from efficiency to resilience requires new production relations that can adapt and withstand shocks.

Black Lake Technologies’ financing history illustrates how financial capital can identify, cultivate, and amplify new quality productive forces. Founded in 2016, the company successfully completed its Series C financing in 2021, and its development path closely aligned with the logic of capital.

Tools provide the foundation. In its early stage, the company’s SaaS software primarily helped individual firms reduce costs and improve efficiency. It later became a platform by connecting people and progressively linking tens of thousands of factories, generating network effects. The sector-wide knowledge accumulated by the platform lowered the barriers and costs of digital transformation for individual firms. With further accumulation, the platform evolved into an industrial ecosystem, co-developing rules with banks, investment institutions, assessment companies, and other participants. At this stage, Black Lake’s role extended beyond efficiency improvement and increasingly took on a rule-shaping dimension.

Summary

A review of Black Lake Technologies’ financing history shows a consistent willingness to pursue technological innovation, continually adding momentum to market expansion and then building and refining its industrial-ecosystem footprint. Point, line, and plane form an integrated three-part development model that reflects the resilience of new quality productive forces.

What is new lies in the organizational form: the model breaks through firm boundaries and creates an open, collaborative community of innovation—a new form of production organization. Resilience lies in systemic capacity: when firms within an ecosystem share data and build capabilities together, the entire industrial chain becomes significantly better able to respond to uncertainty.

How, then, can new quality productive forces be developed? Black Lake offers a potentially useful example. It starts with technological breakthroughs to build the digital foundation of a new form of productivity—this is the “new.” Through innovation in factor allocation, it establishes financial channels serving high-efficiency, high-quality activity—this is the “quality.” Finally, by building an industrial ecosystem, it creates a resilient form of industrial organization capable of responding to a complex environment—this is resilience. Developing new quality productive forces is therefore a system-wide undertaking in which technology, factors, and industry catalyze one another in a spiral of progress.

Five Concrete Forms of Finance

The Recommendations explicitly call for vigorous development of “technology finance, green finance, inclusive finance, pension finance, and digital finance.” Through efficient data aggregation, precise data distribution, and real-time data coordination, Black Lake has addressed information asymmetries in traditional production management. A successful industrial digital ecosystem requires these five forms of finance to interact and reinforce one another as an integrated whole.

A. Technology Finance and Digital Finance

Technology finance addresses the risks inherent in innovation, while digital finance addresses information asymmetries in discovering value. The former “incubates”; the latter “amplifies.”

Technology finance:

Black Lake’s financing journey from its angel round to Series C is a typical example of technology finance. Venture capital was willing to invest when the company was moving from zero to one, pricing technological and market uncertainty. Financial institutions thereby assumed part of the early-stage cost of cultivating the “seeds” of new quality productive forces.

Digital finance:

The “Black Lake Small Work Order” system moves the full process—from sales orders and raw-material procurement to shop-floor production and finished-goods shipment—to the cloud. The fixed procedures of workers scanning codes to report production and warehouse staff scanning goods in and out provide banks with a verifiable source of production data. Because data are collected through actual business processes, they are more likely to be authentic and continuous. Using real-time, trustworthy production-flow data supplied by the Black Lake platform, financial institutions can forecast firms’ future operating cash flows more precisely and price risk more accurately.

Technology finance incubates innovators in digital technology. Once mature, the digital ecosystems built by these innovators in turn provide real-world settings for digital finance. The two create a positive-sum feedback loop: technology finance nurtures digital technology, digital technology empowers digital finance, and digital finance feeds back into technological innovation.

B. Inclusive Finance and Green Finance

The Recommendations call for directing more financial resources toward inclusive and green activities. Under traditional models, however, the cost of serving small and micro enterprises (inclusive finance) or identifying environmental benefits (green finance) can be high, potentially resulting in market failure.

1. Inclusive finance

Why is inclusive finance difficult? The main problems are the costs of risk identification and operations. Through functions such as data aggregation and coordination among multiple roles, Black Lake’s manufacturing system makes production processes more transparent through data and algorithms. Banks can observe key indicators, including equipment-utilization rates and maintenance conditions. These indicators provide an objective basis for assessing a firm’s quality-control capabilities and thus its production stability and continuity. Once the operating efficiency of small and medium-sized firms on the platform is quantified and recognized through data, they may, in principle, obtain credit on a more market-oriented and sustainable basis. This would shift inclusive finance from an administrative or ethical imperative toward an economic-efficiency approach.

2. Green finance

By optimizing production scheduling, reducing inventories, and improving first-pass yield, the Black Lake system increases economic efficiency and also generates positive environmental externalities by reducing wasted energy and pollution from defective products. These green benefits are not yet directly priced. If green finance had precise, verifiable points of entry and a basis for pricing, firms’ efforts toward green transformation could receive tangible financial returns, helping to develop green finance.

C. Implications

The Recommendations focus on deeper reform of the financial system and call for improving the inclusiveness and adaptability of capital-market institutions. From the perspective of inclusiveness, Black Lake’s ability as an industrial-software company to obtain favorable financing is itself a clear signal from the capital market. When capital markets become more inclusive and can identify and support firms whose business models differ substantially from traditional ones but represent future directions, they can more effectively support the development of new quality productive forces.

Conclusion

At the beginning of the new journey under the Fifteenth Five-Year Plan, continued financial reform, more efficient resource allocation, and the development of a digital ecosystem that supports knowledge integration may be viewed as one way to strengthen the foundations for new quality productive forces while following the objective laws of economic development. It follows that continually improving the effective combination of financial resources and knowledge factors is important to building a resilient modernized industrial system in China.

Research context

This paper develops its central question through a defined mechanism, explicit methodological boundaries, and a concluding account of its public significance.
