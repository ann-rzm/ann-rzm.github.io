4:T4e0,What the two parties trade in the “social market” are fundamentally different capital goods. As shown in Table 1, in the constructed model, the publisher is expected to pay a large amount of content (approximately 150 posts) as consideration, covering different emotional types. The interactor’s consideration is designed primarily as a large number of low-cost liking behaviors (approximately 110 times), i.e., a ritualized symbolic behavior. The key lies not in the disparity of quantity but in the mismatch of “currency” types. The publisher attempts to trade “emotional equity,” i.e., expecting a deep relationship; while the interactor pays “social currency,” i.e., maintaining a low-risk option, similar to a “social option.” This mismatch is exposed most starkly in the most extreme data: when the publisher offers “goods” that require no response—positive content (deep thought, interest sharing)—the “transaction” proceeds smoothly (interaction rate 70%–100%); when the publisher offers “goods” that are genuine vulnerability requiring responsibility—the “market” instantly fails (interaction rate 0%).
