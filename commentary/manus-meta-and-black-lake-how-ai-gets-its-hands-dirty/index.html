Manus, Meta, and Black Lake | How AI Gets Its Hands Dirty

Disclaimer: The following does not constitute factual statements or investment advice—only some immature thinking notes.

The hottest news these past two days is undoubtedly Manus and Meta. I tried using this model. First impression: solid, pragmatic, treating individual users as small teams. Everything quantified, process visualized—user-centric. Traditional software interaction, like search engines, has master-slave relationships—commands and reactions. In the AI era, products like ChatGPT are advisory—questions and suggestions. But there’s a gap. Manus’s breakthrough is in principal-agent relationships. This is closer to complex human collaboration. The user is the principal—defining goals, calibrating, judging. AI is the agent—planning, executing, and obligatory reporting. Most importantly: it has long-term, project-tracking functions.

In fact, this model’s temperament—one person equals a team—is similar to their founding team model. Manus has a core of three (Xiao Hong—strategy/resources, Li Yichao—tech, Zhang Tao—product/scenarios). It’s a highly collaborative, ultra-efficient mini-team, trimmed of all redundancy. AI agents supplement human capacity, amplifying individual capability. The product naturally divides clear roles: Planner, Executor, Verifier. Trust-based delegation, outcome-oriented delivery.

For workers: having ideas is good, but how to manually operate is the problem—knowing is easier than doing. Manus solves a major pain point: directly delivering actionable success—delegation equals completion. It flattens the execution gap; efficiency rises.

Main point: What does Zuck (Meta) truly value?

Absolutely not a better ChatGPT. Manus solves persistence. If AI is just a chat partner, it’s just an app. But if AI can permanently hold a working directory, manage API keys, log into websites and act on your behalf—it is the operating system itself. Zuck used to monopolize traffic and extract a “content tax.” But times have changed; traffic no longer flows through feeds, but through agent streams. If all tasks are completed in a sandbox like Manus, Meta loses definition of user behavior. So value doesn’t matter; position does. Before agent interaction logic solidifies, occupying this position itself is more important than short-term financial returns.

Is it really worth that price? That depends on how you define “execution.” LLMs are rapidly open-sourcing; walls are shrinking. But Manus solves AI’s “no hands” problem. This environment scheduling, PEV decomposition of complex tasks is currently closest to real labor. Its commercial essence is selling “certain results.” Those who deliver results always have higher pricing power than those who only provide advice. In AGI, first-mover advantage may only last months. Once an interaction logic (like Manus) becomes global user muscle memory, competitors can only make “compatible versions.” A 3-person team creating billions in valuation is the kind of species mutation giants fear—a virus that could disrupt big-tech organizational logic. It must be confined to a lab, or used for its own organizational blood transfusion.

Is Manus a good comrade or a premium bubble? Three questions mentioned before:

Who bears non-transferable risk? Currently, Meta’s shareholders. Agent-delivered results‘ correctness is probabilistic—if an error causes huge losses, currently unhedgeable.

Where’s the floor? Can it truly leave the pristine demo environment and handle real non-standard tasks? This distinguishes a high-end IDE from a digital employee.

If the logic is understood, what remains? If because others are stupid, it will be flattened by human wave tactics. Its only moat is time and psychological intuition—an intuitive feel that can’t be replicated by hiring 1000 programmers.

Finally: liberating hands or reshuffling? When a tool becomes as reassuring as one’s own hands, “human” actually becomes a “junior auditor” (hhhh). Having lost execution ability, only judgment remains. If your judgment (ability to define goals) isn’t strong enough, you’ll soon be replaced by more advanced agents. Critical thinking, subjectivity, thinking ability—more important than ever. Meta wants future equity; Manus founders take high cash now. This is a swap of certainty and possibility. Manus wins by precisely capturing human (especially bosses‘) greed of “wanting both control and delegation.” This isn’t derogatory—it’s real, even smart.

On Black Lake and the Underlying Reshuffle

Just saw Liu Yuan (ZhenFund)‘s interview—feels like there’s a shared thinking pattern. Understood Black Lake in October because of a symposium. Putting it with Manus, the logic is the same: efficiency-improvement surplus value.

Black Lake: manufacturing’s biggest pain point isn’t no business—it’s chaos.Manus: the biggest obstacle to getting things done is triviality.Whether inside a factory or in the human mind, there’s a connection gap. Black Lake sends production instructions directly to execution ports; Manus: you give it a task. Everything has a response, everything has an outcome—solving trust and supervision problems.

Investing in people—what does it mean? It means whether they have the practicality to get their hands dirty. Going into factories, crouching in workshops, dealing with grease-stained workers—pretentious self-interested “elites” can’t do it. Manus’s obsessive “execution logic” isn’t as flashy as parameter tuning, but solves the most problems. Good leverage, no empty talk.

“No investigation, no right to speak.” “Look up at the stars, keep feet on the ground.”

Some Insights

If an investment opportunity needs others to repeatedly “sell” you on it, it’s already commonplace. Real wise ones are fighting to get in. Don’t put all chips in one basket. Use identity, patience, bugs to hedge. All grand narratives without execution closed loops are fraud. Any return that can’t say who bears it is an illusion. There’s only one Xiao Hong; you can’t imitate him. But the efficiency logic of “one person equals a team” can be used directly.
