The market is not only a barometer of the economy. It is a mirror of human nature — a tsunami of the collective subconscious.

Let one deviant exist, and whoever follows the rules loses out. Wishful thinking from the top fails; coercion in the reverse direction is needed. So a third party outside the conflict — able to monopolize violence and execute punishment — becomes necessary.

Two rational individuals compete for a scarce resource. With no third party, they resolve it privately: endure, or retaliate. Retaliation without consequences never ends, so effective retaliation must becertain, predictable, and publicin order to establish authority. Who guarantees it? People outsource it to a relatively fixed entity. That title might be “Auntie Wang” (rural China) or “Secretary Zhang” (China under rule of law). The ideal is Auntie Wang wearing Secretary Zhang’s hat.

But if people bypass the third party and keep retaliating privately, who guarantees authority? So private retaliation is declared illegal, and upgraded into public retaliation — adjudication. This is a legal order built on deterrence, with punishment as the primary driver. Yet administration is troublesome; as members and behavior types multiply, management costs rise sharply. Deterrence alone is too expensive, so the fix has to move to the root — the willingness to obey: make compliance advantageous to the compliant.

From the legal perspective: people obey the law because they believe it is legitimate — procedural justice, being treated with respect. This is the advanced form of the previous driver; Tyler confirmed it with large-sample research. The other side of the data: an extremely high “sense of obligation to obey” paired with mediocre institutional support, r ≈ 0.25 — public cognition is schizophrenic.

Two authorities, then: Rousseau and Machiavelli — the former abstract, the latter executable. The essence of the social contract is the complete transfer and monopolization of the right of retaliation. Machiavelli says the threat to order comes from those whodisregardthe rules — not oppose, but disregard; because an internal violator at least acknowledges the rule, while disregard is a negation of the rule itself.

So why does an anarchist die by accident? Morality did not kill him. The law did not kill him. Rousseau would say he is logically unprotected — the system serves only citizens.

Between mechanical causation and human agency there is tension and contest, not one-way substitution.
